# A material the teacher designed, into the course

> Talk to the human in the conversation's language, not in this skill's English: every short line between tool calls too. What you publish follows the course's language.

The design is the teacher's: your part is getting it to students in the form that serves them,
in the place it belongs, without losing it on the way. How to export is the design extension's
skill, how to publish the classroom extension's: load both, and this one first.

## 1. Which design, and what it's for

Find the design the teacher means (by its name, the unit, when they made it) and confirm it if
there's more than one that fits. Then decide, with them if it isn't obvious, what students do
with it, because that decides the format and the resource:

| What it is | Format | In the course |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A worksheet or a handout to print or fill in | PDF | A file resource, set to open or download |
| A poster or an infographic to look at | An image (PNG for sharp text) | Inside a page or the unit's text, with alternative text |
| Slides the teacher projects or students review | PDF to read; the slides' format only if students must edit them | A file resource |
| A video or an animation | Video | A page with the video, or a link to where it's published |

Check the formats the design allows before promising one, and say what will be exported (which
pages, which format). If the design has elements the teacher's plan can't export, say which and
let them decide.

## 2. Keep the file

Export links expire: download the file at once to `drafts/<slug>/` (the drafts toolbox), named
by what it is, not by the export's random name, and check its real type (`drafts_info`). That's
the copy you publish and the one a later session finds. Record it in the unit's `topic/` page.

## 3. Before publishing

Look at the material as a student would (`publish-check`): legible at a phone's width, alternative
text for an image, nothing personal about any student (names, photos, marks) in a design made
for a class. Write the line that presents it to students: what it is and what to do with it
("Ficha del tema 3: rellénala antes de la práctica del jueves").

## 4. Publish

In the unit the teacher said, in the place that follows the unit's order, **hidden first**, with
the teacher's yes before it (the approval names the design, the format, the unit and the
resource). Then check it as a student and ask to show it, as `publish-check` says. A design that
replaces an older version of the same material replaces it in place, with its own approval.
