# Attending to diversity

> Talk to the human in the conversation's language, not in this skill's English: every short line between tool calls too. What you publish follows the course's language.

What this skill says about the classroom applies to the connected one, if any; without one, keep it in the knowledge base or build it in `drafts/`.

Diversity is designed in from the start, not patched on for one student. The design reaches
most of the group; what's left is an adaptation the teacher assigns. A need of a named student
is personal, often sensitive data: it never goes in the knowledge base, in `drafts/` or in a page
name.

## 1. Universal design for learning, first

When designing a unit or an activity (`unit-building`, `activity-building`),
check its three principles, and add what's missing:

- **Engagement**: choice where it doesn't change the criterion (the topic of a text, the context
  of a problem), relevance to the students, clear goals, feedback along the way.
- **Representation**: the same content in more than one form (text with headings, a diagram, a
  worked example, a short video with captions), vocabulary explained, accessible materials
  (`publish-check`: structure, alternative text, contrast).
- **Action and expression**: more than one way to show the criterion (written, oral, a
  diagram, a program), scaffolds that can be removed (templates, checklists, partial examples).

## 2. Access adaptations

They change how a student reaches or shows the learning, not what's assessed: more time, a
different format, larger text, an oral instead of a written answer, the statement read aloud,
fewer items with the same criteria. Prepare them as **variants** of the activity, named by what
they change, never by who they're for: "variante con más tiempo", "versión en formato accesible".
The teacher decides who gets each one.

Giving a variant to some students only (restrictions by user or group, extra time for some) is
the teacher's: miyagi doesn't change group membership. Build the variant hidden, and tell the
teacher how to assign it in the classroom.

## 3. Reinforcement and extension, not individual adaptations

Reinforcement and extension activities are open to the whole class: the same criteria worked
again with more support, or further. Prepare them as variants like the above.

Individual curricular adaptations (changing a student's criteria or level) are out of miyagi's
scope (ADR-017): they need a student's personal, often sensitive data, and they follow the
school's own procedure and documents. Say so if asked, and offer the general measures instead.

## 4. Where it's kept

- In the knowledge base, only what's about the course: the UDL measures of each unit (in its
  `topic/` page) and the variants that exist, by what they change.
- The variants' material, in `drafts/<slug>/`, named by what they change.
- Who needs what: nowhere in miyagi. If the teacher tells you in the conversation, use it for
  that conversation only, and don't write it down.
