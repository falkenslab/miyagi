A week with teacher‑agent

You ask for things the way you'd ask a colleague, in a conversation. It works in your Moodle the way you would.

  1. Monday

    Grades the assignment

    It applies your rubric and your criteria, writes feedback that says what went wrong and how to improve, and shows you each student's grade before saving it.

    "Grade Assignment 2 with the unit's rubric"
  2. Tuesday

    Answers the forum

    It finds the unanswered questions, replies in the tone you chose, and tells you when the same question keeps coming back because the notes fall short.

    "Anything new in the forum?"
  3. Wednesday

    Builds an activity

    A quiz, notes, a lab or something bolder, like an escape room. It tries it out hidden in Moodle and shows it to you before your students see it.

    "Make a loops review escape room in Unit 3"
  4. Thursday

    Sums up the class

    Submissions, grades, who's falling behind and which topics are hardest, from what's in your course.

    "How is the class doing overall?"

You decide what gets published

It works with your account in your real course, so it's built not to act on its own where your students would see it.

It asks first

Before saving a grade, replying in the forum or publishing a resource, it shows you exactly what it's about to publish and waits for your yes. If you say no, it doesn't publish it.

It tests before showing

Whatever it builds goes up hidden first, gets tried out in Moodle as a teacher, and needs your yes again to be shown. Anything still hidden, it reminds you of when you close.

It stays in your course

Its instructions are not to delete anything, not to touch enrolments or Moodle's settings, and not to enter other courses. Your password never goes through the model.

It remembers your course

It keeps notes as it goes: your grading criteria, the rubrics, the questions that keep coming up, how the class is doing. The next session starts from there, without going through everything in Moodle again.

They're plain text files in your course's folder, which you can open and correct with any editor. It keeps no files on individual students, only trends across the class.

Talks your language, publishes in the course's

Its menus and messages come in English, Spanish, French and German, and it replies in whatever language you write to it in.

What it publishes in Moodle always follows your course's language: you can talk to it in English about a course taught in Spanish, and its forum replies will be in Spanish.

What it really looks like

A real session on a test Moodle, in English, about a course taught in Spanish: the summary is for you, the reply it quotes is for the students.

Install it in three steps

On Windows, Mac or Linux. No programming needed.

  1. Install Google Chrome

    If you don't have it yet, from google.com/chrome. miyagi uses it to work in Moodle.

  2. Install Node.js

    The LTS version from nodejs.org, with the default options.

  3. Install miyagi

    Open a terminal (PowerShell on Windows, Terminal on Mac), paste this line and press Enter:

    npm install -g https://github.com/falkenslab/miyagi/releases/latest/download/miyagi.tgz

    Then run miyagi init in the folder you want to use for your course and answer its questions.

You need a Moodle course where you have the teacher role, and a Claude Pro or Max subscription. The step-by-step guide (in Spanish) covers everything with examples.

Questions

How much does it cost?

miyagi is free and open source. What runs the assistant is your Claude subscription (Pro or Max).

Does it work with my school's Moodle?

It works with the Moodle you already use, through the browser and your teacher account: nothing to install on the server. While setting it up, it can look (without changing anything) at which activity and question types your Moodle supports.

Could it publish something without me noticing?

In the chat, before saving a grade, replying in the forum or publishing content, it asks your permission with a summary of what it will publish. If it tries to publish without having asked, that's stopped and you're asked.

What about my students' data?

It reads what you would see in Moodle to do its work, but its notes keep no files on individual students, only trends across the class. Your Moodle password is never sent to the model.

Can I give it my own instructions?

Yes. Write whatever it should always keep in mind in an instructions.md file in the course's folder: "grade out of 10", "keep forum replies short", "spelling counts for 10%".
